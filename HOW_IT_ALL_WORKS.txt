PART 1: THE BIG PICTURE (What is the problem?)
===============================================================================

The government measures inflation using something called CPI (Consumer Price
Index). CPI tracks the price of a "basket of goods" — basically a list of
things the average American buys (groceries, rent, gas, medical care, etc.).

THE PROBLEM: CPI calculates ONE number for everyone. But not everyone buys
the same things!

  - A 70-year-old retiree spends a LOT on medical care and almost nothing
    on student loans or restaurants.
  - A 25-year-old renter in Indianapolis spends almost 30% of their money
    on rent and barely anything on medical care.
  - A farmer in rural Indiana spends a ton on gasoline and diesel for their
    trucks and equipment.

So when the government says "inflation was 8%", that might be true for the
AVERAGE person, but some people experienced 14% inflation and others only 4%.

OUR ANALYSIS creates a custom inflation number for each group — that's the
"Divergence Index." It shows how far each group's REAL inflation diverges
(moves away) from the official average.


===============================================================================
PART 2: THE THREE GROUPS (Who are we studying?)
===============================================================================

The case gives us a fictional Midwest bank. We picked three customer groups
that represent very different spending patterns:

GROUP 1: FIXED-INCOME SENIORS (65+)
  - Median income: ~$37,000/year (2025)
  - Main expenses: Medical care (26% of spending!), groceries, home energy
  - Why they matter: They live on Social Security and pensions — their income
    barely rises, but their costs keep going up. Medical costs are their #1
    pain point.

GROUP 2: URBAN RENTERS (young working professionals)
  - Median income: ~$48,000/year (2025)
  - Main expenses: Rent (28% of spending!), food, education/student loans
  - Why they matter: Rent is their biggest cost by far. When rent goes up,
    they have very little room to adjust.

GROUP 3: RURAL SMALL BUSINESS OWNERS
  - Median income: ~$60,500/year (2025)
  - Main expenses: Gasoline/diesel (12%), energy (12%), property, vehicles
  - Why they matter: Their businesses are energy-intensive. When gas and
    diesel prices spike, their operating costs explode — way more than the
    average person.

PART 3: THE METHOD (How did we calculate it?)
===============================================================================

STEP 1: Get price data
  We pulled REAL price data from the Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS).
  This is the same data the government uses. We got prices for 17 categories
  (groceries, rent, gas, medical care, electricity, etc.) from 2021 to 2025.

STEP 2: Figure out how much each group spends on each category
  Using the BLS Consumer Expenditure Survey (a massive survey of what
  Americans actually buy), we estimated what percentage of their money each
  group spends on each category.

  Example:
    Seniors spend 18% on medical care, but Urban Renters only spend 4%.
    Urban Renters spend 28% on rent, but Seniors only spend 6%.
    Rural Biz spends 12% on gasoline, but Seniors only spend 4%.

  These percentages are called "expenditure weights."

STEP 3: Calculate each group's personalized inflation rate
  For each year, we take every category's price change and multiply it by
  how much that group spends on it, then add them all up.

  THE FORMULA (in plain English):
    Group's inflation = (weight_groceries x grocery_price_change)
                      + (weight_rent x rent_price_change)
                      + (weight_medical x medical_price_change)
                      + ... (for all 17 categories)

  REAL EXAMPLE — Rural Small Biz in 2022:
    Gas went up 27%. They spend 12% of income on gas.
    Energy went up 20%. They spend 12% on energy.
    Just those two: (0.12 x 27) + (0.12 x 20) = 3.24 + 2.4 = +5.64%
    ... from gas and energy ALONE, before even counting food, rent, etc.
    Their total: +14.4% (vs. the official 8.0%)

STEP 4: Calculate the divergence
  Divergence = Group's inflation rate  -  Official CPI rate
  Rural Biz 2022:  14.4% - 8.0% = +6.5 percentage points ABOVE average

  That "+6.5pp" is the Divergence Index for that group in that year.

PART 4: THE RESULTS (What did we find?)
===============================================================================

DELIVERABLE 1: THE DIVERGENCE INDEX

  Year  |  Official CPI  |  Seniors  |  Renters  |  Rural Biz
  ------|----------------|-----------|-----------|------------
  2022  |     +8.0%      |  +10.1%   |  +10.4%   |  +14.4%
  2023  |     +4.1%      |   +2.1%   |   +2.7%   |   +0.2%
  2024  |     +3.0%      |   +2.1%   |   +1.7%   |   +0.6%
  2025  |     +2.6%      |   +2.9%   |   +2.4%   |   +2.1%

  KEY STORY:
  - 2022 was the BIG year. ALL groups paid more than the average, but
    Rural Biz got absolutely hammered (+14.4%, nearly double the average).
  - After 2022, energy prices cooled down, so Rural Biz actually paid LESS
    than average in 2023-2025 (they benefited from the price reversal).
  - Seniors have been CONSISTENTLY above or near average because medical
    costs keep rising steadily — they don't get relief like energy groups do.
  - From 2021 to 2025, total cumulative inflation was about +17-18% for
    all groups, meaning everything costs ~18% more than it did in 2021.


DELIVERABLE 2: NON-DISCRETIONARY BURDEN

  "Non-discretionary" means things you MUST pay for — you can't just stop
  buying groceries, paying rent, or going to the doctor. These are fixed
  costs. "Discretionary" means optional things — eating out, entertainment,
  new clothes.

  We calculated: What % of each group's income goes to must-pay expenses?

  Group               |  2021  |  2025  |  Change
  --------------------|--------|--------|--------
  Seniors             |  82.0% |  84.5% |  +2.5pp
  Urban Renters       |  62.0% |  65.8% |  +3.8pp
  Rural Small Biz     |  76.0% |  82.3% |  +6.3pp   <-- BIGGEST SQUEEZE

  WHAT THIS MEANS IN REAL MONEY (2025):
  - Seniors earn $37,000/yr. After essentials, they have ~$5,735 left.
    That's $478/month for EVERYTHING else — savings, fun, emergencies.
  - Rural Biz earns $60,500/yr. After essentials, ~$10,709 left.
    Sounds like more, but they went from 76% to 82.3% burden — the biggest
    jump of any group (+6.3 percentage points).
  - Urban Renters earn $48,000/yr. After essentials, ~$16,416 left.
    They're the least squeezed, but rent still eats 28% of their income.

  THE MOST SQUEEZED: Rural Small Business Owners.
  Their burden jumped 6.3 percentage points in just 4 years.


DELIVERABLE 3: WHAT SHOULD THE BANK DO?

  We recommend a "Divergence-Adjusted Credit Framework" (DACF) — basically,
  stop using the average inflation number and start understanding which
  customers are ACTUALLY under stress.

  THREE PILLARS:

  1. FIX THE RISK MODELS
     Current credit models assume everyone experiences the same inflation.
     Wrong. Add demographic inflation exposure to how the bank evaluates
     loan risk. When a group's Divergence Index spikes, flag those loans
     for closer monitoring.

  2. CREATE TARGETED PRODUCTS
     - Seniors: Inflation-indexed CDs (savings accounts that pay more when
       medical costs rise), prescription financing at 0% interest
     - Renters: Rent-smoothing accounts, first-time homebuyer programs
     - Rural Biz: Energy cost hedging, flexible seasonal payments

  3. STOP LOSING DEPOSITS
     The bank lost $150 million in deposits (2021 to 2025). People are
     pulling money out because they need cash to survive. Target each group:
     - Seniors want SAFETY → guaranteed-rate CDs
     - Rural Biz want FLEXIBILITY → fee waivers on business accounts
     - Renters want GROWTH → high-yield savings with autopay bonuses

PART 5: THE BANK'S DATA (Connecting inflation to credit stress)
===============================================================================

  The case gives us these bank numbers:

  Metric                    |  2021     |  2025     |  Change
  --------------------------|-----------|-----------|--------
  Total Deposits            |  $1.2B    |  $1.05B   |  -$150M (people took money out)
  Auto Loan Default Rate    |  1.4%     |  2.6%     |  almost doubled
  Credit Card Utilization   |  29%      |  41%      |  people maxing out cards
  Small Biz Delinquencies   |  1.1%     |  3.2%     |  TRIPLED

  THE CONNECTION:
  - Energy prices spiked in 2022 → Rural Biz owners burned through savings
  - By 2024-2025, they couldn't keep up → delinquencies tripled
  - Across all groups, people withdrew deposits to cover rising costs
  - Credit card usage spiked because people needed to borrow to survive
  - Auto loan defaults rose because car + gas costs hit rural areas hardest

  This is the whole argument: the "average" inflation hides who's hurting.
  The Divergence Index reveals the truth, and the bank can use it to make
  smarter decisions.

PART 7: KEY VOCABULARY (For your presentation)
===============================================================================

  CPI (Consumer Price Index)
    The government's official measure of inflation. Tracks prices of a
    "basket of goods" that the average American buys.

  Inflation
    When prices go up over time. If inflation is 8%, something that cost
    $100 last year now costs $108.

  Basis point (bp) / Percentage point (pp)
    A way to measure changes in rates. 1 percentage point = 1%.
    So going from 1.4% to 2.6% is a +1.2 percentage point increase.

  Non-discretionary spending
    Things you HAVE to buy — food, housing, medical care, gas, electricity.
    You can't just decide to stop eating or stop paying rent.

  Discretionary spending
    Things you CHOOSE to buy — restaurants, entertainment, new clothes.
    When money gets tight, these are what people cut first.

  Expenditure weights
    What percentage of their total spending a person puts toward each
    category. Example: if you spend $280/month on rent out of $1000 total,
    your rent weight is 28%.

  Divergence Index
    Our custom metric. It's the difference between a group's real inflation
    and the official CPI average. Positive = they paid MORE than average.

  Default rate
    The percentage of loans where borrowers stopped paying. Higher = bad
    for the bank.

  Delinquency rate
    The percentage of loans where borrowers are LATE on payments (but
    haven't fully stopped paying yet). A warning sign before defaults.

  Credit card utilization
    How much of their available credit limit people are using. 29% is
    normal. 41% means people are getting desperate and maxing out cards.

  TIPS (Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities)
    Government bonds that adjust their value based on inflation. Banks
    can use these to hedge (protect themselves) against inflation risk.

  CRA (Community Reinvestment Act)
    A law that requires banks to serve ALL communities in their area,
    including low-income ones. Our strategy helps with CRA compliance.

PART 8: HOW TO RUN THE CODE
===============================================================================

  The entire analysis runs from the project folder:

    cd ~/divergence-index

  To see the dashboard (the interactive web app):
    streamlit run app.py

  To re-run the full pipeline (pulls fresh data + recalculates everything):
    python3 run_pipeline.py

  To re-run WITHOUT pulling new data (just recalculate):
    python3 run_pipeline.py --skip-pull

  Project structure:
    divergence-index/
      app.py                          <-- the dashboard (what you see in browser)
      config.py                       <-- all settings, weights, bank data
      run_pipeline.py                 <-- runs everything in order
      data/
        pull_bls_cpi.py               <-- pulls price data from government
        pull_fred.py                  <-- pulls economic data from Fed
        raw/                          <-- downloaded data files
        processed/                    <-- calculated results
      analysis/
        divergence_index.py           <-- the math (calculates the index)
        executive_summary.py          <-- generates the text report
      visualizations/
        charts.py                     <-- generates standalone chart files

        executive_summary.txt         <-- full written report
